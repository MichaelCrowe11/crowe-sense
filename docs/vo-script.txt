How to record: one long take in Voice Memos is fine. Quiet room, phone 6 to 8 inches from your mouth, Lossless quality if the setting is there. TIER ONE: 18 lines. These carry every film. About five minutes.

1.  Crowe Sense.

2.  Crowe Sense is a sensing node for a growing room. A Raspberry Pi 5, four Sensirion and Bosch sensors under a louvered radiation shield, a 3D-printed housing, about $280 in parts. It reads CO2, temperature, humidity, light and volatile compounds, and it keeps every reading on the node.

3.  A signed copy of every reading goes to a relay, so the same numbers show up in the desktop app, the web app, the phone, Cortex, the terminal, and any screen in the room. One contract, six surfaces.

4.  This is what a grower sees. Temperature, humidity, CO2 and VPD for every room, the flow hood, and the health of the controller itself. The numbers on this screen are demonstration data; the real ones come from the pilot.

5.  Readings are judged against 43 condition bands taken from my own cultivation videos, each cited to the clip it came from. A band needs two independent statements before it judges anything. Nobody else can ship that layer, because nobody else has the corpus.

6.  The software is built and tested end to end. 105 tests across the firmware, the relay and the apps, all green. The parts list is priced. The enclosure is a parametric model, ready to print.

7.  What is not proven is the room. No node has yet run with physical sensors in a growing room. The pilot changes that. Twenty-five nodes in ten growers' rooms, and every node's first week of data passes a provenance test before anyone calls it working.

8.  I have grown mushrooms since 2005, about ten of those years commercially, and roughly 195,000 people follow the channel where I teach it. I built every surface you just saw.

9.  The raise is $175,000 on a SAFE. Cloud and analytics are already covered by credits, so the money is hardware and people. Day 30, five nodes in real rooms. Day 90, twenty-five nodes at ten growers. Day 180, twenty labelled harvests.

10.  The pilot budget is $175,000. Twenty-five nodes, a contract hardware engineer, and a year of the founder's time. Cloud and analytics are covered by credits the company already holds. Every milestone carries its own proof.

11.  This is the sensor I have been building for grow rooms, and the software around it is done. Here is what it is, what it is not yet, and how you can get one in your room.

12.  The code, the firmware and the enclosure are public on GitHub, MichaelCrowe11 slash crowe-sense. If you run rooms and want a node in one of them for the pilot, the link is in the description. Michael Crowe, Crowe Logic, Phoenix.
